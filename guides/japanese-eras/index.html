How Japanese Era Dates Work

Why your residence card application asks for "令和8年" instead of 2026 — and how to convert era dates without looking anything up.

What is a Japanese era?

Japan officially uses two calendar systems side by side. Alongside the Western (Gregorian) calendar, official documents use the era system, called gengō (元号). An era name is chosen when a new emperor accedes to the throne, and years are counted from 1 within that era. 2026 is Reiwa 8 (令和8年) — the 8th year of the Reiwa era, which began in 2019.

You will run into era dates constantly in everyday paperwork: driver's licenses, health insurance cards, bank forms, city-hall certificates (juminhyo, koseki), tax documents, and visa applications all routinely use them. Birth dates on official records are usually written in the era active when you were born — so someone born in 1990 is "Heisei 2".

The five modern eras

Since the era system was reformed in 1868, there have been five eras:

EraKanjiBeginsEndsWestern years
Meiji明治Jan 25, 1868Jul 29, 19121868–1912
Taisho大正Jul 30, 1912Dec 24, 19261912–1926
Showa昭和Dec 25, 1926Jan 7, 19891926–1989
Heisei平成Jan 8, 1989Apr 30, 20191989–2019
Reiwa令和May 1, 2019—2019–present

On forms, eras are often abbreviated to a single letter or kanji: M (明) Meiji, T (大) Taisho, S (昭) Showa, H (平) Heisei, R (令) Reiwa. A date like R8.6.12 means Reiwa 8, June 12 — that is, June 12, 2026.

Converting in your head

The formula is simple: Western year = era year + (first year of the era − 1). In practice you just memorize one offset per era:

  • Reiwa: add 2018 (Reiwa 8 → 2026)
  • Heisei: add 1988 (Heisei 15 → 2003)
  • Showa: add 1925 (Showa 50 → 1975)
  • Taisho: add 1911 (Taisho 10 → 1921)
  • Meiji: add 1867 (Meiji 40 → 1907)

Things that trip people up

  • Year 1 is called gannen (元年), not "year 1". Documents from 2019 may say 令和元年 rather than 令和1年 — they mean the same thing.
  • Transition years belong to two eras. 1989 is Showa 64 through January 7 and Heisei 1 from January 8. 2019 is Heisei 31 until April 30 and Reiwa 1 from May 1. For these years, the exact month and day decides which era applies.
  • Era years are not aligned to January. Except for the transition years above, era years do follow the calendar year — but the first and last year of each era are partial years, so "Showa 64" only covers one week.
