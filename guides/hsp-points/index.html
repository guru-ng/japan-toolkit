Understanding Japan's HSP Points System

Japan's Highly Skilled Professional system scores your education, career, salary, and age — reach 70 points and the usual 10-year wait for permanent residency can shrink to 3 years, or even 1.

What the system is

The Points-Based Preferential Immigration Treatment for Highly Skilled Foreign Professionals (高度人材ポイント制) is run by Japan's Immigration Services Agency (ISA). If your circumstances add up to 70 points or more, you qualify for the Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) status of residence, which comes with preferential treatment — most famously, a much faster path to permanent residency (PR).

The PR fast track

  • Standard rule: PR generally requires about 10 years of continuous residence in Japan.
  • 70+ points: eligible to apply for PR after 3 years of qualifying HSP activity.
  • 80+ points: eligible to apply for PR after just 1 year.

Importantly, you must have maintained the score over that period — immigration assesses your points both now and as of 3 years (or 1 year) ago.

The three categories

There isn't one points table — there are three, one per activity type:

CategoryTypical applicantNotes
(a) Advanced academic research University researchers, professors Has a research-achievements item worth up to 25 points; no minimum salary gate
(b) Advanced specialized / technical Engineers, IT professionals, most company employees The most common category; requires annual salary of at least ¥3,000,000
(c) Advanced business management Executives, company managers No age item; salary brackets run ¥10M–¥30M+; position (e.g. representative director) earns points

How points add up (category b)

For the common engineer/specialist category, the main items are:

  • Education: bachelor's 10, master's 20, MBA/MOT 25, doctorate 30 — plus 5 if you hold multiple advanced degrees in different fields.
  • Professional experience: 5 points at 3 years, scaling to 20 points at 10+ years.
  • Annual salary: 10 to 40 points, on a matrix that depends on your age — e.g. ¥10M+ is always 40 points, but a ¥5M salary only scores if you're under 35.
  • Age: under 30 earns 15, declining to 0 at 40+ (younger applicants get more headroom on lower salaries instead).
  • Qualifications & research: national qualifications or designated IT certifications up to 10; research achievements (patents, papers) 15.

Bonus points most people overlook

  • Japanese-language ability: JLPT N1 (or a Japanese-language degree) +15; N2 +10.
  • Japanese degree: graduating from a Japanese university +10 — this stacks with the N1/N2 bonus.
  • Top-ranked university: a degree from a university on the MOJ's designated list (top-300 world rankings, among others) +10.
  • Growth-field work: working on an advanced project in a government-recognized growth field +10.
  • Employer bonuses: innovation-support certified employers +10 (or +20 for certified SMEs), SMEs with R&D spending over 3% of revenue +5.

The language and university bonuses alone can swing a borderline case by 25–35 points, which is often the difference between the 3-year and 1-year track.

Watch out for

  • The ¥3,000,000 salary floor. For categories (b) and (c), an annual salary under ¥3M disqualifies you outright, no matter how many points you have elsewhere.
  • Evidence matters. Every point you claim must be documented — degree certificates, employment records, tax withholding slips, JLPT certificates.
  • The lists change. The designated-university list, growth-field list, and employer-certification lists are updated periodically; always check the current versions on the ISA website before applying.
